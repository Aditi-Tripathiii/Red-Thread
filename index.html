LIVE EXPLANATION

“Your Aadhaar is on the dark web.”

That phrase alone does not prove anything. The real warning signs are what the caller asks you to do next.

Possible impersonationCaller claims to be police and asks you to stay on a video call.
High-risk actionThey ask for an “RBI verification” payment or your OTP.

RedThread response: pause, verify independently, and do not transfer money.

HOW THIS FRAUD WORKS

Fear is the attack. The transfer is the goal.

A scammer may create a fake website, forged notice, or convincing profile. They may claim that someone accessed the dark web, sent a parcel, or misused an Aadhaar number. RedThread looks for connected behavior, not just alarming words.

01

Claim authority

They pretend to represent police, ED, CBI, NCB, TRAI, a court, or FedEx to make the message feel official.

02

Create isolation

They demand secrecy, keep the victim on a Skype or WhatsApp call, or say they are under “digital arrest.”

03

Demand control or money

They request an OTP, remote app, UPI transfer, or “security deposit” into a temporary account.

Check a suspicious claim

Paste a message, website address, or call excerpt. This demo explains the signals; it does not make legal conclusions or automatically report anyone.

What RedThread checks

  • Does the message pretend to be a trusted authority?
  • Does it create urgency, secrecy, or isolation?
  • Does it request an OTP, personal details, a remote app, or money?
  • Does the website name look similar to, but not exactly like, an official domain?
Grounding fact: “Digital arrest” is not a legal process in India. Real authorities do not demand a UPI transfer or a security deposit to verify your bank account. If you are frightened, end the call, tell someone you trust, and contact 1930 if money or information was shared.
RedThread is a safety aid. It explains suspicious signals and keeps the user in control.
